# Stage 2 — Single-mechanism judgment experiment

**Status:** selected experiment plan, SF-0004; not implemented or tested.
**Author:** Mothlight — The Invisible Stagehand
**Decision source:** [six-review reconciliation](../reviews/STAGE0_RECONCILIATION.md), R-03/R-04/R-16/R-18.

## Question and scope

Does accessible evidence let a physically situated stagehand make a consequential choice to intervene or deliberately refrain while a performance advances? Does that choice feel worthwhile?

One bounded Room, one meaningful mechanism (the chandelier restraint candidate), one scheduled Cue, and a small visible performance consequence. A passive performer may communicate the Beat without an NPC AI. Use Move, local timed Inspect and Operate, with nonaction allowing ticks to continue. No autonomous Ghost, Director runtime, Power/Rage economy, binding, psychological meters, generalized dialogue/content loader or multi-Room navigation is required.

This is distinct from Stage 1 visual studies, headless transition proofs and the later integrated Last Waltz Adventure. Headless fixtures share the same rules but cannot establish embodied comprehension or fun. The later Adventure's duration, mechanism count, cast and binding scope remain hypotheses.

## Paired authored conditions

| Property | A — Authorized revision | B — Ordinary fault |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial symptom | Unexpected-looking movement near a Cue | Similar unexpected-looking movement |
| World cause | Valid revised operation with safe restraint/configuration | Unsafe physical configuration with an ordinary cause |
| Available evidence | Accessible effective revision and mechanism observations consistent with safe operation | Reachable mechanism evidence inconsistent with the safe configuration |
| Justified choice | Allow the valid effect when the evidence warrants it | Perform a feasible corrective operation or appropriate safety action |
| Consequence | The intended Beat visibly succeeds; unnecessary interference causes a proportionate lost effect/delay | Timely correction prevents the specified failure; delay/nonaction permits its physical consequence |

These are required distinctions, not finalized geometry, dialogue, timing, machinery engineering or a generated answer key. Author the actual mechanical states before implementing them. Each condition must declare what can be known, how, when, at what access/time cost and with what reliability. Do not choose a preferred answer and manufacture an arbitrary penalty for the alternative.

## Information and causality rules

- Keep intended rehearsal/revision, actual world state and participant observations distinct. Evidence is a projection of accessible facts, not another world authority.
- Inspect reveals condition or evidence; it does not return an omniscient normal/fault/Ghost label. Existing notes may be read without acquiring new facts while paused.
- Operational danger can be understood before causal responsibility. The eventual binding investigation must not be a prerequisite for ordinary competent intervention.
- The response must fit the actual traversal, inspection and operation opportunity. No universal reaction-time value is selected here. Severe hazards require R-04's fairness evidence; the first toy need not introduce lethal damage merely to test judgment.
- A false intervention's cost follows physical or dramatic rules. Informed caution may legitimately sacrifice a flourish or cancel a performance. Outcomes remain multidimensional.
- A successful prevention stays successful. A cue revision cannot be invented retrospectively to invalidate the player's evidence.
- The pair is trial coverage, not a promise that every eventual Run contains exactly one false alarm. Avoid teaching participants a scenario-count shortcut.

## Planned technical checks — not run

1. Identical complete inputs produce the same authoritative events in supported headless/browser hosts; frame cadence is irrelevant.
2. A supports successful restraint and a causal cost for unnecessary intervention; B supports a feasible intervention that changes its physical result.
3. Remote or repeated invalid inspection cannot reveal inaccessible facts; valid Inspect consumes the declared ticks and publishes observations at the declared time.
4. Cue and player operations sharing a tick obey the completed conflict rule, without duplicate effects.
5. Required evidence remains usable in each supported Shot/input/sensory treatment. Art parity and perceptual validity are checked separately.
6. Explicit pause/suspension does not advance the world or trigger catch-up on resume.

## Planned human observation — not run

Pre-register the question, case conditions, available information, comparison policies, recording/consent procedure and stopping rule. Pilot to calibrate timing and sample/decision thresholds, then evaluate revised designs using held-out equivalent cases. No success percentage or reaction-time constant is justified today.

Compare evidence-informed decisions against always-intervene, always-refrain and indiscriminate interaction strategies. Record condition and exposure order, evidence available/encountered, commands and effective ticks, chosen action or restraint, consequences, the participant's causal explanation/confidence and experience of fairness/enjoyment. Keep evaluator coaching and after-the-fact explanations distinguishable. Asking for concurrent explanation can itself consume attention; document the collection method and its effect.

Use matched initial symptoms and affordances, counterbalance exposure, and account for reading speed, theater knowledge, prior learning, visual/audio/input mode and latency. Logs alone cannot establish why someone acted. No hidden telemetry is introduced by this plan.

## Continue, revise, and limits

Continue only when the evidence supports meaningful, understandable choice and enough player interest to justify another small experiment. If available observations cannot distinguish the cases, repair the fixture before interpreting outcomes as player failure. If interaction degenerates into guessing or repetitive clicking, make a controlled clarity/accessibility correction and re-evaluate; persistent failure returns us to the observation/action design before adding Ghost complexity.

A one-mechanism test cannot prove attention allocation across competing machinery, sustained dread, the value of Ghost strategy, long-run replayability, or a complete game. Inspecting the only mechanism may be rational; that alone is not a failure. Later transfer cases must establish whether interpretation generalizes beyond memorizing one clue. A negative result diagnoses its specific scope, not the whole game's impossibility.

Fifth Bell remains required in the larger design. Its request/acknowledgment and late-cancellation semantics receive a separate bounded fixture before affected implementation; physical cast evacuation and save/restart are not hidden prerequisites for this minimal pair. A restart-only toy must disclose that limitation. Later add a matched Ghost-caused disturbance to test whether a legally constrained adversary adds tension.

## Before implementation

Complete the Room/Shot geometry, actual mechanism transitions, effective revision evidence, action/time policy, same-tick precedence, supported controls/modalities and trial protocol. Obtain acceptance of the relevant design gate and a scoped implementation task. Stage 0 documentation does not itself start Stage 2.
