# Stage Fright — Planned Gameplay, Version 0

**Status:** a falsifiable *game design*, not code or a claim of playtested fun.

## The game promise

You're a physically present stagehand in a haunted theater. The House wants to wreck the production, frighten or injure the cast, and—if it can—kill you. You can attempt to finish the scheduled Play, protect people and cancel responsibly, or learn enough to bind the haunting. A successful show and a successful banishment are independently scored outcomes.

The player should feel watched by an adversary that observes their repeated choices through defined senses. The visible show is a production with real cues and real legitimate effects; suspicion alone is not evidence of sabotage.

## The heartbeat

**Observe → Interpret → Intervene or refrain → Witness.**

Typical repeated actions: cross a Room, check the next Cue, notice an odd sound, Inspect a mechanism, compare the clue with its expected state, Operate or deliberately leave it alone, then watch the consequence. The show clock keeps running. All important equipment uses the same small verb vocabulary: **Move, Inspect, Operate, Communicate, Use/Place**.

**Sources of enjoyment to test:** recognition, meaningful uncertainty, physical cause-and-effect, improving mastery, well-timed intervention, and expressive performer recovery. Speed alone isn't the reward. The player may succeed by doing nothing because the apparent haunting was a legitimate Cue.

**Failure mode to avoid:** repeatedly clicking suspicious props until an arbitrary meter fills. An Inspection should reveal useful physical evidence or eliminate a plausible hypothesis.

## Overlapping strategic loops

| Loop | Recurring player question | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Showkeeping** | How do I keep the intended or an acceptable improvised performance running? | Maintain Cues, equipment and performer confidence. |
| **Rescue / survival** | Who or what will be hurt next, and how do I prevent it? | Redirect hazards, warn the Cast, tend injuries, decide whether to stop. |
| **Investigation** | Which events are natural, rehearsed or ghost-caused? | Infer senses, anchor, constraints and viable binding setup. |
| **Binding** | Can I trap the House without abandoning everyone onstage? | Prepare containment, force a Power crisis, hold until resolved. |

The same Move/Inspect/Operate/Communicate/Use vocabulary applies to all four. A scenario does not have to use every loop; an Adventure can emphasize them differently.

## The ghost's opposing loop

**Perceive → choose legal objective → commit/spend Power → attempt physical outcome → resolve → earn reward or Rage → recover.**

The Ghost has a regeneration/Power economy and an opponent's survival problem of its own. Waiting earns Power slowly. Pulling off attributed outcomes can earn capped Power. Being thwarted gives Rage, accelerating future Power restoration. Every committed action costs Power even when stopped. Director pacing governs opportunities and recovery but may not conjure new damage or reward failure. Power at zero does **not** kill an unbound ghost; effective containment stops regeneration and progressively drains it.

The attack surface is physical: a loosened restraint, altered lamp cue, jammed door, or frightening manifestation. A cheaply released mechanical load may be lethal because of stored ordinary energy. Characters/props suffer damage from the world, not abstract Ghost HP attacks.

## Cast and dialogue are part of the gameplay

An Actor has a body, fears, knowledge, relationships and agency. A Role has rehearsed lines and obligations. Actors can hesitate, improvise, continue despite fear if they trust the crew, withdraw despite composure if they judge equipment unsafe, or become incapable through injury.

One scripted Play Scene should allow at least these **authorable** reactions:

1. **Rehearsal holds:** standard line delivered with its intended Cue.
2. **Ordinary deviation:** late light or legitimate revised Cue, requiring cover dialogue or a meaningful pause.
3. **Ghost interference:** a physically observable anomaly yields alarm, cautious improvisation, or warning—never knowledge the Actor could not have.
4. **Rescue or equipment loss:** a different line salvages the dramatic Beat using an available replacement effect.
5. **Breakdown:** the Role refuses or cannot continue; the Show Controller selects a declared fallback, cancellation or failure.

No LLM dialogue. Select stable authored line variants by visible context, local knowledge, priority and deterministic tie-break. Backstage speech should contrast with in-character performance; silence is an authored outcome.

## The two truths every run must preserve

**Rehearsal truth:** What the Play and Production intended to happen; ordinary faults and approved revisions are possible.

**World truth:** What actually happened and why; the Ghost may have interfered or may have done nothing. The player sees partial evidence, while debugging/replay can inspect the whole causal chain.

A normal lighting effect must sometimes resemble sabotage. A false intervention must be capable of harming the show. Otherwise the mystery collapses into a red-highlighted repair checklist.

## Outcome model

Keep independent dimensions rather than flattening all results into a single score:

- **Performance:** finished intact, improvised, interrupted, canceled or collapsed.
- **People:** living, injured, incapacitated, missing, or rescued.
- **Haunting:** active, exhausted, bound, expelled, or destroyed (where authored).
- **Player:** alive, escaped, injured, or dead.

The **Fifth Bell** is a deliberate evacuation/cancellation option. It may lose the performance while saving lives. The Ghost's destruction is never a mandatory win condition for every Adventure.

## The smallest conceptual demonstration — The Last Waltz

The initial candidate Play is one Act with a few dramatic Scenes. Its Venue offers a compact group of Rooms; its Stage has a chandelier restraint, curtain mechanism and light cue. Mara, one Actor assigned to a dancer Role, has a rehearsed line plus contingency dialogue. A bounded Ghost can move a control or threaten a physical chain reaction; at least one suspicious event is genuinely harmless or mundane.

**Example decision:** The chandelier moves before expected. The player can cross the wing to inspect the latch or interrupt the performance immediately. They may discover a ghost tampering, an ordinary loose fastener, or an authorized cue revision. Resolution depends on world state and timing, not designer fiat.

**Candidate outcome routes:** safely finish the waltz; complete an improvised ending after a damaged prop; evacuate; suffer failure/injury; or choose a discovered containment route if that scenario authored it.

## Stage 0 acceptance examples (specification only)

- A returning player recognizes an early warning and acts sooner; the Director doesn't invent a replacement.
- An apparent anomaly turns out to be legitimate, and correcting it makes things worse.
- The Ghost spends Power on a legal action, the player thwarts it, the spent Power stays spent, and Rage increases later regeneration.
- A frightened but trusting Actor continues; a calm, injured Actor cannot.
- An alternate line covers a missed Cue without revealing knowledge the Actor lacks.
- An incapacitated Actor never performs a required Beat through a hidden script exception.
- The binding route and the survival route can yield different combinations of valid outcomes.
- A different Art Pack changes the visible Room/Shot but not the causal event trace.

**No balance constants, controls, dialogue texts, Play scene counts or acceptance metrics have been ratified yet.**
